# Veiday Game Concept

## Working Title

**Veiday**

A VEILIGHT fan-made daily incremental idol-life sandbox about preparing spaces, leaving suggestions, watching autonomous days unfold, and growing toward a finite-feeling grand stage before continuing into an endless cozy postgame.

Korean pitch:

```txt
Veiday는 장막 너머의 스페이스를 꾸미고 작은 제안을 남기며,
베이라이트 그룹이 관계, 방송인으로서의 저명성, 아이돌로서의 성장을 쌓아
언젠가 큰 무대에 도달하는 과정을 지켜보는
데일리 인크리멘탈 샌드박스 팬게임이다.
```

## Core Thesis

The protagonists are **VEILIGHT as a group**.

The player is not the protagonist, producer, boss, or owner. The player is a fanlike helper beyond the veil who prepares spaces, leaves suggestions, gives gifts, and observes what happens.

Core rule:

```txt
Do not command. Suggest, prepare, observe, and remember.
```

The player can:

- prepare spaces
- decorate rooms, stages, gardens, islands, and booths
- leave suggestions
- give gifts
- set the mood
- deliver fan letters
- place tools, props, lights, tiles, and memories
- observe autonomous behavior
- record photos and daily logs

The player cannot directly force:

- a broadcast
- a song
- a schedule
- an outfit
- a collaboration
- a relationship
- a mood
- a result
- movement to a specific space

This is one of the most important ideas borrowed from **Tomodachi Life / Friend Collection** style play: characters have autonomy. The player creates conditions, but characters choose how to respond. Like watching a tiny Sims-like household, the player reads behavior and adjusts the environment rather than directly puppeteering every action.

## Genre

Veiday is closest to:

- daily incremental game
- cozy idol-life sandbox
- autonomous character observation game
- room/stage/island decoration game
- light atmospheric management sim
- finite-feeling story progression with endless postgame
- fan ritual game

Reference influences:

- NEEDY GIRL OVERDOSE: internet broadcast atmosphere, stats, notifications, online pressure
- Chinese Parents: long-term life-growth structure
- Volcano Princess: character growth over time
- Princess Maker / Magical Princess: nurturing and parameter growth with endings
- Idol Manager: idol/fandom/activity systems
- Tomodachi Life / Friend Collection: autonomous characters, suggestions instead of commands, daily observation
- The Sims: watching autonomous daily life, nudging through environment and objects rather than direct control
- incremental games: endless accumulation, upgrades, daily return loops

Veiday should not become a hard command-based management game. It should feel more like a living miniature world that responds to the player's care over time.

It also has a Sims-like observation feeling: the joy is not only in optimizing outcomes, but in watching small autonomous routines, habits, reactions, and accidents emerge from the environment the player prepared.

## Player Fantasy

The player is closer to:

- a fan
- a small helper beyond the veil
- a keeper of tiny lights
- a room/stage caretaker
- an observer who leaves notes and gifts

The fantasy:

```txt
I prepare a small place beyond the veil.
I leave light, props, letters, tiles, and suggestions.
Then I watch what kind of day VEILIGHT chooses to have.
```

## Main Journey and Endless Veiday

Veiday should be able to continue forever, but it should not initially feel like a directionless endless idle game.

The game should present a finite-feeling main journey:

```txt
small room -> first spaces -> relationships -> growing recognition -> idol growth -> grand stage ending
```

After the ending, the game continues as **Endless Veiday**:

- daily check-ins
- tile collection
- tile extraction and synthesis
- space expansion
- photo albums
- relationship events
- seasonal events
- decoration collection
- recurring autonomous days

Design rule:

```txt
The game can be endless, but the player should feel like they are moving toward a meaningful destination.
```

### Possible Main Goal

A large milestone such as:

- Veiday Grand Stage
- Starlight Festival
- first large solo live
- anniversary stage
- a major fan community milestone

The exact goal is not final, but it should combine:

- relationship growth
- streamer recognition
- idol growth
- space development
- memory collection

### Chapter Direction

Possible chapter structure:

1. A small room beyond the veil
2. First suggestions and first daily report
3. Opening the first additional Space
4. Meeting randomly generated outsiders
5. Building streamer recognition
6. Preparing idol/stage growth
7. Expanding toward a festival or grand stage
8. Ending / Grand Stage
9. Endless Veiday unlocked

## Growth Axes

Veiday's main growth should come from three large axes.

### 1. Relationships

VEILIGHT can form relationships with randomly generated outsiders.

Potential outsider types:

- fans
- regular viewers
- other streamers
- artists
- composers
- staff-like helpers
- neighborhood-like abstract NPCs
- creative partners
- rivals
- mentors

Relationship types can include:

- fan
- friend
- collaborator
- mentor
- rival
- regular viewer
- creative partner

The player does not force relationships. Spaces, tiles, moods, suggestions, and events create opportunities. VEILIGHT chooses how relationships develop.

Example:

```txt
A well-kept fan letter space attracts a random fan NPC.
Repeated visits may create a regular viewer relationship.
That relationship may later produce fanart, a clip, a letter, a collaboration hook, or a memory.
```

### 2. Streamer Recognition

VEILIGHT grows as streamers through community, broadcasts, fan rituals, clips, and online presence.

Possible values:

- Viewers
- Followers
- Fan Warmth
- Buzz
- Trust
- Community Size
- Stream Identity

Example milestones:

- first regular viewer
- first fan letter
- first fanart
- first viral clip
- first collaboration
- first community meme
- first major broadcast moment

Recognition should not be only raw numbers. It should also express what kind of community is forming.

### 3. Idol Growth

VEILIGHT grows as idols through stage presence, music, performance, confidence, and expression.

Possible values:

- Vocal
- Stage Presence
- Expression
- Repertoire
- Live Confidence
- Costume Sense
- Motion / Dance
- Performance Spark

The player cannot command practice. Instead, they can prepare music/stage spaces, leave song suggestions, place microphones, set lighting, or provide inspiration resources. Idol growth events emerge from those conditions.

## Core Loop

### Short Loop: Seconds to One Minute

- collect small light resources
- inspect room/stage/space reactions
- place or move a small prop or tile
- read a tiny notification or fan reaction
- see autonomous character behavior
- take a snapshot-worthy moment

### Medium Loop: Five to Fifteen Minutes

- prepare a mood or room setup
- offer one or more suggestion cards
- place tiles into spaces
- leave fan letters or items
- watch a resulting activity, refusal, variation, or surprise
- gain light, memories, inspiration, relationship progress, or fandom energy
- unlock small decorations

### Game-Day Loop

This is an internal game day, not necessarily a real-world day.

- prepare spaces
- place tiles
- leave suggestions
- press **Spend Day / 하루 보내기**
- simulate autonomous visits and activities
- receive a day report
- gain rewards and mission progress

### Real Daily Loop

Real-world daily play exists, but it must not block main story progress.

- check in
- receive real daily rewards
- receive random tiles or tile choices
- recover missed-day rewards through mini-games
- preserve a daily log

### Endless Loop

- expand spaces indefinitely
- collect decoration sets
- grow fandom/light levels
- collect memory cards
- build photo albums
- improve passive generation through environment quality
- keep returning because the world keeps having new small days

## Pre-Ending Progression

Before the main ending, Veiday should not feel like a slow real-time daily game.

The player should be able to progress quickly through story missions and **Spend Day**.

Pre-ending tile and system acquisition should come from:

- story mission rewards
- chapter rewards
- first-discovery rewards
- relationship events
- streamer recognition milestones
- idol growth milestones
- day reports
- real daily check-in as a bonus only

Design rule:

```txt
Before the ending, real daily rewards are bonuses, not progression bottlenecks.
```

The player should be able to rapidly experience:

- tile placement
- space expansion
- suggestions
- autonomous visits
- relationship growth
- recognition growth
- idol growth
- day reports
- first photo moments

## Spend Day / 하루 보내기

**Spend Day** advances the internal game date.

It should simulate the current setup:

- spaces
- tiles
- moods
- suggestions
- relationships
- VEILIGHT's current condition
- random whims
- active story missions

Then it produces a report.

Example report:

```txt
오늘의 Veiday

- 루루엘이 별빛 정원에서 팬레터를 읽었어요.
- RED레드가 미니 스테이지의 마이크를 살펴봤어요.
- 시아가 새로 놓은 쿠션에서 잠깐 쉬었어요.
- Fan Warmth +12
- Inspiration +4
- 새 타일 획득: 작은 스탠드 조명
- 미션 “첫 무대 준비” 진행도 +1
```

Spend Day exists so the main journey can move at the player's pace instead of being locked to real calendar days.

## Suggestions, Not Commands

Actions should be framed as suggestions and preparations, not commands.

Prefer these verbs:

- suggest
- offer
- prepare
- leave
- place
- invite
- gift
- decorate
- set mood
- deliver
- tidy
- observe
- record

Avoid command-like verbs:

- order
- force
- assign
- schedule directly
- make them do X
- control

### Example: Broadcast Activity

Command-style design to avoid:

```txt
Start singing stream.
```

Veiday-style design:

```txt
Place a microphone.
Turn on warm stage lights.
Leave a song request note.
Prepare tea near the desk.
```

Possible autonomous outcomes:

- they start a singing stream
- they hum briefly but do not stream
- they practice off-camera
- they read the request note
- they ignore the microphone today
- they rest because mood or condition is low
- they take a cute photo with the microphone prop

Failures should not feel like failures. They should feel like characterful responses.

Example response:

```txt
오늘은 그냥 별빛 아래서 멍때리고 싶은 모양이에요.
```

## Tiles

Tiles are the basic unit connecting growth, decoration, and space expansion.

The player gains tiles, places them into spaces, extracts unwanted tiles into materials, and synthesizes materials to target desired tile types.

Design summary:

```txt
Tile = a small piece of the world.
Space = a themed area made from tiles.
Tile placement = an environmental suggestion.
```

Tiles should not be only floor pieces. They can represent:

- floor and terrain
- furniture
- lights
- props
- stage equipment
- fan letters
- windows
- gardens
- broadcast equipment
- drawing boards
- memory objects
- atmosphere objects

Each tile may have:

- visual appearance
- tags
- mood influence
- resource influence
- autonomous behavior hooks
- photo value
- adjacency effects
- extraction materials

### Tile Tags

Possible tags:

- cozy
- stage
- music
- rest
- fan
- night
- star
- digital
- cute
- red
- blue
- garden
- desk
- memory
- art
- stream

Tags influence:

- which spaces feel coherent
- which characters visit
- which activities become likely
- which resources are generated
- which photos/events can occur
- which synthesis recipes are possible

### Daily Tile Acquisition

Real daily check-in may grant:

- random tile
- tile pack
- choose 1 of 3 tiles
- date-themed tile
- missed-day tile echo

But pre-ending story progression should grant tiles much faster than real daily check-ins.

### Extraction and Synthesis

Unwanted tiles can be extracted into tag materials.

Examples:

```txt
Microphone tile -> music shard + stage dust
Cushion tile -> rest shard + cozy dust
Starlight tile -> star shard + light dust
Fan letter tile -> fan shard + memory bit
```

Synthesis lets the player target desired tile categories.

Example:

```txt
music shard + stage shard + light dust
-> higher chance for music/stage/light tiles
```

This keeps random daily rewards from becoming frustrating. Even unwanted tiles become progress toward a desired space.

## Spaces

A **Space** is an independent small area made from tiles.

Examples:

- Home Space
- Mini Stage Space
- Starlight Garden Space
- Practice Room Space
- Cafe Space
- Drawing Room Space
- Fan Letter Archive
- Broadcast Booth
- Dream Island

Spaces prevent infinite map expansion from becoming visually chaotic. Each space can have a coherent concept, mood, and purpose.

Each space may have:

- name
- concept
- tile grid
- dominant tags
- mood
- beauty / atmosphere values
- visit history
- autonomous behavior hooks
- camera presets
- photo framing presets
- level or development state

### Space as Environmental Invitation

Characters should not be sent to spaces by command.

Instead, they choose spaces based on:

- current mood
- needs
- curiosity
- energy
- interests
- relationship hooks
- active suggestions
- space tags
- recently placed tiles
- random whim

Example:

```txt
A tired character is more likely to visit cozy/rest spaces.
A music-focused mood increases the chance of visiting stage/music spaces.
A newly placed prop may attract curiosity.
Fan-related events may lead to visits to fan letter spaces.
```

Thus, decorating a space is an indirect invitation.

```txt
Space design = a soft invitation, not a movement command.
```

## Sims-Like Observation

Veiday should preserve the pleasure of simply watching.

The player may prepare a room, leave objects, set a mood, place tiles, then spend time observing what VEILIGHT naturally does with it. A good session can be valuable even if the player does not optimize anything.

Important observation moments:

- someone notices a newly placed prop
- a room setup changes idle behavior
- a small habit repeats across days
- an unexpected interaction creates a snapshot moment
- a suggestion is ignored, reinterpreted, or turned into something else
- the player learns preferences by watching instead of reading a stat sheet
- a character moves between spaces according to mood
- a random outsider relationship appears naturally

This should feel less like controlling units and more like tending a tiny living diorama.

## Missed Days and Day Echoes

Veiday should not punish players harshly for missing real daily check-ins.

If the player misses a real-world date, that date can leave behind a small recoverable memory such as:

- Day Echo
- 하루의 잔광
- missed-day memory
- forgotten light

The player can select the missed date and play a short mini-game to recover that day's reward.

Possible mini-games:

- collect scattered starlight
- sort fan letters
- adjust stage lights
- restore a doodle/sticker
- assemble tile fragments

Design rule:

```txt
A missed day is not a failure. It is a faint light that can be picked up later.
```

Recovered missed-day rewards can include:

- the missed daily tile
- Light
- tag materials
- memory bits
- small decorations

Streak rewards, if any, should stay mostly cosmetic to avoid pressure.

## Incremental Design Direction

The incremental system should feel like an ecosystem, not a factory.

The player does not press buttons to directly produce resources. Instead, better environments and better care increase the chance, quality, and frequency of autonomous reactions.

Examples:

- cozy lighting increases passive Light generation
- organized fan letters increase Fan Warmth
- microphones and music props increase chance of music-related events
- rest spaces improve recovery and reduce burnout-like pressure
- displayed drawings or stickers increase memory/photo events
- decorations affect mood, activity preferences, and snapshot aesthetics
- coherent spaces attract more relevant visits
- relationship events improve recognition or inspiration

Possible resources:

- Light: basic soft currency
- Starlight: rarer milestone currency
- Fan Warmth: fandom affection / support
- Hype: broadcast/event momentum
- Inspiration: art/music/idea energy
- Memory: album and story unlock resource
- Mood: room atmosphere value
- Tile Shards: materials from extraction

Resource names are not final, but they should feel atmospheric and fanlike rather than industrial.

## Story Missions

Story missions guide the finite-feeling main journey.

They should teach and reward systems without becoming direct commands.

Good mission examples:

```txt
Prepare a Mini Stage Space with 3 stage/music tiles.
Place a fan-letter tile in any cozy space.
Leave your first suggestion card.
Create a space with a strong night/star mood.
Reach a streamer recognition milestone.
Help a randomly generated regular viewer become part of the community.
Collect enough memories to unlock the next chapter.
```

Avoid missions like:

```txt
Make someone sing.
Force a broadcast.
Send a specific character to a specific room.
```

Missions should reward tiles generously before the ending, so players can build spaces quickly and understand the sandbox systems.

## Decoration and Snapshot Priority

Veiday should be highly screenshot-friendly.

Important features:

- cute room/stage/island decoration
- tile-based space composition
- props and stickers
- lighting and atmosphere controls
- camera pan/zoom/framing
- hide UI for screenshots
- photo mode
- album/memory log
- shareable 1:1, 16:9, and vertical compositions later
- drawing-board or sticker-board style user-made decorations
- camera presets per space

The world should often produce moments that make the player want to take a picture.

## Tone

Target tone:

```txt
cute, daily, atmospheric, soft, slightly lonely, warm, internet-broadcast-adjacent
```

The game can borrow the UI language of livestreaming, notifications, fandom, and online rituals, but it should avoid becoming harshly exploitative or too dark.

Important emotional mix:

- warm fan support
- tiny room at night
- soft lights
- the feeling of checking in every day
- fast early progression toward a meaningful goal
- small autonomous surprises
- a gentle sense of distance beyond the veil
- the cozy voyeurism of watching tiny autonomous routines unfold
- post-ending comfort and collection

## Relationship to VEILIGHT

Veiday is a fan game inspired by VEILIGHT concepts such as:

- veil / 장막
- light / 별빛
- virtual streaming
- stage and broadcast atmosphere
- fandom rituals
- daily check-ins and support
- group growth and idol-like aspiration

The game should avoid implying official status unless explicitly authorized.

Safer fan-game framing:

- use original abstracted mascots, lights, rooms, props, and symbols where possible
- avoid directly commanding or controlling real members/personas
- make the player an observer/helper/fan rather than an owner or producer
- make VEILIGHT the protagonists, not player-owned units
- include clear unofficial fan-game wording when public-facing materials are added

## Naming Notes

### Chosen Working Title: Veiday

Reasons:

- short and memorable
- connects to VEILIGHT through `Vei`
- connects to daily check-in and routine through `day`
- fits daily progression and autonomous observation better than a pure stage title
- supports both finite story journey and endless postgame
- less command/management-coded than idol-manager style names

Other considered names:

- Veilstage: strong for stage/live/broadcast focus, but less daily-life oriented
- Behind the Veil: atmospheric and lore-friendly, but heavier and less cozy
- Pet Your Light: poetic and cozy, but less tied to VEILIGHT/fandom systems
- Veigotchi: clear pet/incremental reference, but too close to pure pet-care framing
- Maeilight: meaningful but harder to read/pronounce

## Summary

```txt
Veiday is a VEILIGHT fan-made daily incremental idol-life sandbox.
VEILIGHT is the protagonist.
The player cannot command; the player prepares, suggests, observes, and records.

Before the ending, story missions and Spend Day provide fast progression and generous tile rewards.
The group grows through relationships, streamer recognition, and idol growth toward a large finite-feeling goal.

Tiles are the basic world pieces.
Spaces are coherent themed areas built from tiles.
Characters autonomously move between spaces based on mood, needs, tags, novelty, relationships, and whims.

After the ending, the game becomes Endless Veiday:
daily check-ins, missed-day mini-games, tile collection, extraction, synthesis, space expansion, photo albums, and cozy observation.
```
